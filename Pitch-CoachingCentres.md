# Pitch playbook — Private Coaching Centres

**Owner:** Deepak · **Draft:** 27 Sep 2026 · **Status:** v0.1 — rehearse, run 3 live meetings, then edit this file with what you actually heard.

> A pitch is not a monologue. This document gives you the words to open with,
> the demo to show, and the objections to expect. What closes the meeting is
> whether you listen well in the middle.

---

## 0. The one insight everything hangs on

**Every coaching-centre owner's #1 fear is star-teacher dependency.** When a top faculty member leaves, results dip that year, admissions dip the next year, and the brand takes 24 months to recover. Every owner has lived through this or watched a competitor die from it.

You are not selling recording software. You are selling **star-teacher insurance**.

Everything below flows from that. If the owner does not nod when you name this pain in the first 60 seconds, either the pain isn't sharp yet (small centre, no star teacher) or the owner isn't the right ICP — move on.

## 1. The three-minute pitch (chance encounter / phone opener)

Use when: someone hands you 3 minutes at an event, a friend introduces you at a wedding, or you get a phone call back from a cold email.

### The words (memorise these — do not read)

> "Bhaiya, quick question — what happens to your Physics batch if [name of their top teacher] decides to open his own centre next April?"
>
> *[Pause. Let them answer. They will laugh or wince. Either is fine.]*
>
> "We're building GyanSaar for exactly that. It's an app your teachers use in class — they tap one button, teach normally, tap stop at the end. What comes out is a transcript of the lecture, an approved lesson record, and a fresh set of practice questions from that specific class. The teacher approves everything before it goes anywhere. Nothing leaves without their sign-off.
>
> The reason I'm calling is not to sell you anything today. We're picking two coaching centres in NCR for a four-week pilot at no cost. In return, we'd like your honest reaction. Would you have 20 minutes next week to see it work?"

### Why this pitch works

- **First line names their fear in their language.** Not "faculty retention risk", but "what happens if [Name] leaves". Specific > abstract.
- **"You tap one button, teach normally"** — the biggest hidden objection is workload. Killed in 6 words.
- **"Teacher approves everything"** — the biggest ethical objection is surveillance. Killed in 4 words.
- **"Two centres, four weeks, no cost, we want your honest reaction"** — the ask is small, time-bound, and flatters them as an expert. Very hard to say no to.

### What you must NOT say

- **"AI-powered"** — dilutes trust. Say "app" or "software". They'll figure out it's AI.
- **"Analytics dashboard"** — kills interest instantly. They don't want dashboards; they want teaching.
- **"Improve teaching quality"** — insults their teachers. You're helping distribute what their teachers already do well, not fix them.
- **"Modernise your operations"** — patronising.
- Anything about Byju's, Unacademy, etc. Do not compare yourself to failed brands even by contrast.

## 2. The 30-minute first meeting

Use when: you have the owner sitting across from you.

### 2.1 First 3 minutes — rapport, not pitch

- Ask what board/exam they focus on (JEE / NEET / Boards / CUET / CAT).
- Ask how many centres and how many teachers.
- Ask what result they're proudest of. **Listen for a teacher's name.** That's your leverage.
- Do not open a laptop. Do not slide a deck. This is a conversation.

### 2.2 Minutes 3–8 — the pain reframe

> "Can I share how I see the coaching-centre business right now, and you tell me where I'm wrong?"
>
> *[They will always say yes. Anyone will agree to hear their own business explained.]*
>
> "You have three or four teachers who genuinely move the needle. Everyone else is competent, but those three or four are the reason parents pick you. Your P&L is basically their P&L. When one of them takes a bad phone call from a competitor, you lose sleep for a week.
>
> Now — you can't clone them. But what if you could capture how they teach, not just what they teach? Not videos — nobody watches those. What they *actually said*, searchable, in a form your other teachers can study and your students who missed class can catch up from. And what if the same recording gave you a fresh set of practice questions matched to that lecture, ready for a WhatsApp broadcast?
>
> That's GyanSaar."

### 2.3 Minutes 8–18 — the demo

**Rule of demos: show the wow moment first, explain the mechanism second.**

Sequence:

1. **Open the app, tap "Start Recording" on a class.** Say: "I'm your teacher. I've just walked into a Physics batch." Put the phone in your pocket. Keep talking to the owner.
2. Talk for 45–60 seconds about anything — the weather, the meeting, whatever. This is the demo audio.
3. **Take out the phone, tap Stop.** The app shows: "Uploading… done."
4. **Open the approval screen.** Show the transcript. Say: "This is what the teacher actually said, word by word."
5. **Show the auto-generated practice questions.** Say: "The app generated these from what the teacher said. Nothing is published to students until the teacher taps Approve. If a question is wrong, teacher edits it or deletes it — takes 15 seconds. If a whole recording was rubbish, teacher taps Discard and it's gone forever, nothing counts against them."
6. **Tap Approve. Show the 'Copy for Classroom' button.** Say: "Now the teacher pastes this to the batch's WhatsApp group. Students who missed class have the notes. Students who came have the practice set."
7. **Show the queue** — the teacher's dashboard of all their recordings this week.

Total demo: 8 minutes if smooth.

### 2.4 Minutes 18–25 — the pilot ask

> "We're not asking you to buy anything today. We're picking two coaching centres in NCR for a four-week pilot. Here's what we offer and here's what we ask in return.
>
> **What we offer:**
> - Free access for four weeks, up to 40 hours of recording per teacher.
> - We personally set it up on your teachers' phones — takes 10 minutes per teacher.
> - Weekly check-ins with you and your Academic Head.
> - Full data export at the end, whether you continue or not.
>
> **What we ask:**
> - Two of your teachers, two of your batches, four weeks.
> - One 30-minute call with each teacher after week 2.
> - Your honest reaction at the end — either 'this is worth paying for' or 'here's why not'.
>
> Is that fair?"

### 2.5 Minutes 25–30 — objections and close

Answer objections (see §4). Then close with **one specific next step**, not "let me know":

> "If this is a fit, I'll email you a one-page pilot agreement tonight and we can start on Monday. Can I have the number of your Academic Head to loop them in?"

**The number is the close.** If they give you the AH's number, you have a real pilot. If they say "let me discuss internally", the meeting was polite but you did not sell.

## 3. The demo — exact choreography

The demo is the pitch. Rehearse it 20 times before your first live meeting. Notes:

| Beat | What you say | What happens on screen | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Watch — I'm the teacher, this is my phone." | Tap Start. Big red timer starts. | Confidence: it works with one tap. |
| 2 | *[Nothing — talk to owner for 60 sec]* | Screen locks. Timer keeps counting in the notification. | This is the wow: recording survives lock. |
| 3 | "Class done." | Tap Stop. Small progress toast: uploading. | Speed: no waiting. |
| 4 | "Here's what came out." | Transcript appears with timestamps. | Reality: this is what my teachers say? |
| 5 | "And here's what the software suggests as practice." | 5 questions with marks. | Value: this is 30 minutes of teacher work, done. |
| 6 | "Teacher decides — approve, edit, or discard. Nothing goes out on its own." | Tap Approve. Green tick. | Control: teacher is the boss. |
| 7 | "For the batch." | Copy for Classroom button. Preview text. | Distribution: one action reaches parents. |

**Recovery plans if demo breaks:**

- **No internet at their office:** always carry a mobile hotspot. Never demo on someone else's WiFi.
- **App crashes:** have a screen-recorded version of the flow on your phone as backup. Say "let me show you the recorded version — it happens to be the same". Do not apologise more than once.
- **Mic permission blocks:** rehearsed teacher grants mic before you enter the room. Never demo on a fresh install.

## 4. Objection handling — the top ten

### 4.1 "My teachers will refuse to be recorded."

> "You're right to worry about that. The reason we designed it around teacher consent is exactly this — the teacher is the one who taps Start, taps Stop, approves what goes out, and can Discard any recording forever with one tap. Nothing counts against them, no metric measures how much they discard. The teacher owns their tape. Would it help if I met your top teacher for 15 minutes before we start, so they can push me on the same question?"

### 4.2 "What if the recording leaks and my teacher's method reaches a competitor?"

> "This is the exact reason we don't store audio anywhere your teacher's phone or your staff can touch. The raw audio goes to encrypted cloud storage, and even I can't read it — the system won't give me access. Only the transcript and the questions are visible, and only to the teacher, until they approve. Would you like Ankit — our co-founder, engineering — to walk your IT person through it?"

### 4.3 "Is this even legal? Consent, DPDP, all that?"

> "Yes, and here's the specific reasoning. The teacher is an adult employee giving explicit consent to record themselves at work. That's lawful under DPDP. We are not recording individual students — students may be in the ambient audio but the app treats the teacher as the subject, not the class. The transcript is the teacher's IP, held for them. We can share a one-page legal note before the pilot if you want to run it past your lawyer."

### 4.4 "How much does it cost?"

> "For the pilot, nothing. For after — pricing depends on how many teachers and how many batches. We're targeting a range that's less than one teacher-hour per active classroom per month. Cheaper than one extra doubt-clearing session. Let's talk numbers after you've seen it work for four weeks — I don't want you to buy based on my slide, I want you to buy based on your teacher's reaction."

### 4.5 "What about internet? Half my centres have flaky WiFi."

> "The recording is stored on the phone first. If internet drops mid-class, recording continues. When the phone reconnects, the app uploads in the background. Your teacher never sees an error, never loses a class."

### 4.6 "My teachers are not tech-savvy."

> "One button. One tap to start, one tap to stop. If they can use WhatsApp, they can use this. We spend 10 minutes with each teacher on day one, and there's nothing more to learn."

### 4.7 "What if my teacher forgets to record?"

> "Two things — a home-screen widget so it's one tap from lock, and (optional) a scheduled reminder for their next batch. But honestly, the reason a teacher forgets is they don't see the value. That's on us to prove in week one."

### 4.8 "Can we white-label it? Put our brand on it?"

> "Not for the pilot — we want your teachers reacting to the product, not the paint. If you become a paying customer with 20+ teachers, custom branding is on our roadmap for [placeholder — check with Ankit before answering]."

### 4.9 "Can it integrate with my existing LMS / student portal?"

> "Right now — no. Roadmap — yes, if you're a customer with a specific LMS. Tell me which one and I'll come back with a timeline."

### 4.10 "How is this different from just having teachers record on Zoom or their phone?"

> "Recording is 10% of the value. The other 90% is what happens after — transcript, practice-set generation, approval workflow, one-click distribution to your batch. A Zoom recording is a 2-hour video no student ever re-watches. This is a searchable transcript, a fresh problem set, and a WhatsApp-ready note. Different product entirely."

## 5. The follow-up email (send same day, within 4 hours of meeting)

Subject: **GyanSaar pilot — quick recap and next step**

> Dear [Name],
>
> Thank you for the time this morning. As promised, three things:
>
> **1. The pilot in one page.** [Attach one-pager. If you don't have one yet, tell me — I'll help you draft it.]
>
> **2. What I heard.** You said your biggest fear is [X — quote them back to themselves in their own words]. GyanSaar addresses that by [one sentence]. Please push back if I've misheard.
>
> **3. Next step.** If you're in, please share [Academic Head's] number and I'll set up a 15-minute call to plan the first week. If you'd like to think about it, a Friday callback works for me.
>
> One more thing — I mentioned we'd meet [top teacher's name] before we start. Happy to come to your centre anytime this week or next; a 20-minute cup of chai is enough.
>
> Regards,
> Deepak · GyanSaar · +91-XXXXX-XXXXX

**Why this email works:**

- Names their words back to them. This is the single most powerful move in follow-up.
- Small, specific next step: a phone number, not a decision.
- Offers to meet the teacher — proves you take the consent design seriously.
- Sent within 4 hours. Delay kills warmth.

## 6. Rehearsal checklist before your first meeting

- [ ] Read this document out loud once. Adjust anything that doesn't sound like you.
- [ ] Rehearse the demo 20 times, alone, with the actual phone.
- [ ] Rehearse the demo 5 times with someone else pretending to be the owner.
- [ ] Have a recorded backup of the demo on your phone.
- [ ] Carry a hotspot device with a data pack that has ≥ 2 GB left.
- [ ] Grant mic permission on the demo phone before you leave home. Do a test recording that morning.
- [ ] Charge phone > 80 %. Bring a charger.
- [ ] One-pager pilot terms printed × 2 — for owner and Academic Head.
- [ ] Do NOT bring a laptop unless they've asked for one.
- [ ] Wear what the owner is likely to wear + one notch up. Kurta or shirt with pants, not a suit.

## 7. What to do after each meeting

Within 2 hours, in a Google Sheet:

- Who you met (name, role, phone).
- What they said the pain was (verbatim, in their words).
- What objection surprised you.
- What worked in the demo.
- What did not work.
- Next step you committed to, and by when.

**Then, after 3 meetings:** come back to this file and edit anything that stopped working or wasn't true. This is the point of P0 in the GTM plan — the pitch you take into P1 should be the one that survived the first three real conversations, not v0.1.
