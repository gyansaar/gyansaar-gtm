# Pitch playbook — Grad / Post-graduate colleges

**Owner:** Deepak · **Draft:** 27 Sep 2026 · **Status:** v0.1 — do not use in front of a Dean until Ankit and the founder have read it.

> Colleges do not buy the way coaching centres buy. The person you meet is
> almost never the person who signs. Read §0 twice.

---

## 0. Three insights everything hangs on

### 0.1 You are selling to a champion, not to a decision maker

In a coaching centre, the owner you meet on Tuesday can sign on Thursday. In a college, the Dean you meet on Tuesday needs to convince a committee across six weeks. Your pitch is not designed to close them — it is **designed to arm them**. Everything you say, and everything you leave behind, should be reusable inside a room you will never be in.

Corollary: the leave-behind materials (§5) matter more here than the meeting itself.

### 0.2 The wedge is not AI. The wedge is NAAC and faculty workload.

Deans do not want to be told their institution needs AI. They want to be told:

- Their next NAAC/NIRF submission will have documented teaching-learning artefacts.
- Their faculty will spend fewer hours on assignment-setting admin.
- Their absenteeism problem will improve because absent students can catch up asynchronously.

If those three don't land, no amount of AI-branding will move the deal.

### 0.3 Faculty association pushback is the #1 killer. Have your answer ready before it is asked.

The single most common way a college pilot dies is a faculty representative saying "this is surveillance" in a departmental meeting you're not in. Prepare for this in the first meeting — do not wait for it in the fifth.

## 1. Segment splits — three different sales motions

The "colleges" segment is three different segments in disguise. Do not use the same playbook for each.

### 1a. Private universities (Amity, Sharda, Bennett, Ashoka, Manav Rachna)

- **Champion:** Vice-Chancellor, Pro-VC (Academic), or Dean of a School.
- **Sales cycle:** 6–12 weeks.
- **Procurement:** internal, not GEM. Standard MSA + PO.
- **Speed dependency:** VC's calendar. If they like it, movement is fast.
- **Faculty union:** exists but usually toothless in private universities.
- **Best opening wedge:** their placements story. They market placements; you can position GyanSaar as evidence of teaching quality that recruiters will hear about.

### 1b. Autonomous DU colleges + Jamia (SRCC, LSR, Hindu, Miranda, Stephen's, JMI)

- **Champion:** Principal, or a well-networked Head of Department.
- **Sales cycle:** 3–6 months.
- **Procurement:** highly variable — some autonomous, some through DU central.
- **Speed dependency:** the Principal's political capital with their staff council.
- **Faculty union:** DUTA is a real force. Address it publicly and early.
- **Best opening wedge:** NAAC re-accreditation cycle. Every DU college is in one at any time. Ask "when is your next NAAC?" in the first meeting.

### 1c. Central universities (JNU, University of Delhi central, IIIT Delhi if applicable)

- **Champion:** Dean of Academic Affairs, or a specific School's Dean.
- **Sales cycle:** 6–12 months. Sometimes never closes.
- **Procurement:** GEM portal, government tender rules. You may need to be an empanelled vendor before anyone can even trial.
- **Speed dependency:** procurement calendar, not academic calendar.
- **Faculty union:** JNUTA, DUTA, others — highly organised.
- **Best opening wedge:** honestly, **don't start here.** Come back to central universities in year two, with three case studies from private/autonomous colleges in hand. Attempting central universities as a startup with no track record will consume time you don't have.

**Sequencing recommendation:** private universities first (Amity, Bennett, Sharda), autonomous DU/Jamia colleges second, central universities third.

## 2. The five-minute pitch (email or phone opener)

Use when: you have written access to a Dean/HOD/VC and 5 minutes of their attention.

### The email that opens the door

Subject: **Classroom teaching documentation for [College Name] — 15-minute discussion**

> Dear Professor [Name],
>
> I am writing about a small project we are running with a handful of NCR institutions this term, and would value your reaction even if not immediately relevant to [College].
>
> GyanSaar is a classroom instrument built at consent-first principles: an instructor records their own lecture with one tap, and the system produces a searchable transcript, a documented lesson record, and a first-draft assignment set. The instructor remains the sole approver at every stage — nothing is published without explicit sign-off, and any recording can be discarded with no institutional trace.
>
> Two reasons this may be of interest to [College] specifically:
>
> 1. **Documentation of teaching-learning processes** for NAAC/NIRF cycles.
> 2. **Reduction in faculty admin time** on assignment-setting, which is the most-cited complaint we hear from faculty across NCR.
>
> We are piloting this term with the [Commerce / Economics / Management] departments of [1–2 named comparable institutions, only if truthful]. If a 15-minute conversation with you or a colleague you'd suggest would be productive, I can come to [College] any day next week.
>
> With regards,
> Deepak Nagpal
> GyanSaar
> +91-XXXXX-XXXXX

### What makes this email different from the coaching pitch

- **Formal register.** "Bhaiya" would be an insult here.
- **Names NAAC/NIRF explicitly** in the first two lines. That is the whole wedge.
- **"Even if not immediately relevant"** — humble, opens a door without pressure.
- **Comparable-institutions namedrop** — only include truthfully, and only if pilot is actually in motion or advanced discussion. Do not fabricate.
- **Offers to come to them.** Deans do not travel for startup pitches.

## 3. The 45-minute first meeting

Longer than coaching. Colleges expect a fuller presentation and time for their questions.

### 3.1 First 5 minutes — establish standing

Do not open with the pitch. Establish that you understand their world.

- Ask what programmes their department runs, and roughly what section sizes.
- Ask when their next NAAC/NIRF cycle is.
- Ask what their faculty complains about most in the current term.
- **Listen for one of these three phrases:** "faculty workload", "student absenteeism", "documentation". Any one of these is your hook for §3.2.

### 3.2 Minutes 5–15 — the pain reframe

> "May I share what we hear consistently from HODs across Delhi, and you tell me where [College]'s situation is different?"
>
> *[Yes. Always yes.]*
>
> "Faculty are asked to teach five hours a day, set assignments for every course, evaluate each set, respond to make-up requests from absent students, document their teaching for internal reviews, and — at NAAC time — reproduce evidence of what happened in the classroom eighteen months ago.
>
> There is no single tool that reduces this. Each of these is either done by hand or not done at all. What's typically deprioritised is documentation and absentee support, because those are the invisible ones — until NAAC arrives.
>
> GyanSaar addresses this by capturing what happened in each class, once, at the point of teaching — with the instructor's full control and consent — and then producing three artefacts from that single capture: a lesson record for institutional archives, a searchable transcript for absent students, and a first-draft assignment set for the instructor to edit and issue. Nothing leaves the instructor's approval queue without their sign-off. And the raw audio is inaccessible to anyone but the automated system — not by our staff, not by yours, not by the institution."

### 3.3 Minutes 15–30 — the demo

Same choreography as the coaching pitch, with these adjustments:

| Beat | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Watch — I'm the teacher" | "Imagine I am a member of your Economics faculty." |
| "Class done." | "Lecture concludes." |
| "Copy for Classroom" | "This is what the instructor can share with students who missed today — through your existing LMS or a class group." |
| "Fresh set of questions" | "First-draft assessment set — the instructor edits or discards; nothing is issued without their approval." |

Keep the demo tight — 12 minutes maximum. Colleges have less patience for product demos than coaching centres because the buying decision isn't visual.

### 3.4 Minutes 30–40 — the leave-behind conversation

This is the pitch's turning point. Do not ask for a pilot yet. Instead:

> "Professor, I would like to leave three documents with you so you can share them with whoever else at [College] should see this — a Head of Department, a NAAC coordinator, whoever the right internal audience is.
>
> [Slide across the table:]
> 1. **A one-page product summary** — what we do, what we don't do, how faculty consent works.
> 2. **A NAAC-alignment note** — how the lesson records we generate map to criteria under Curricular Aspects and Teaching-Learning-Evaluation.
> 3. **A one-page data-protection brief** — how we comply with DPDP, where audio is stored, who can access what.
>
> Would it be useful if the next conversation included one or two of your faculty members? I would especially like to hear the concerns of anyone who might be skeptical. That is worth more to us than a room of agreement."

Requesting faculty in the room is the single move that most reliably de-risks the deal. Do not skip it.

### 3.5 Minutes 40–45 — the small ask

> "Would you be willing to circulate these three papers this week and let me know by [Friday, 10 days out] whether a follow-up with your Head of [Department] and one or two faculty members would be productive?"

The ask is **circulation**, not commitment. That is the appropriate first ask for a college champion.

## 4. Multi-touch sequence — plan for four meetings, not one

| # | Who | Purpose | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Dean / Principal / HOD (champion) | Discovery + demo + leave-behind | 45 min |
| M2 | Champion + 2–3 faculty representatives | Consent design deep-dive; hear their objections | 45 min |
| M3 | IT / DPO / procurement contact | Compliance + integration review | 30–60 min |
| M4 | Champion + Registrar or Finance | Pilot terms + commercials | 30 min |

**Rule:** never propose M4 (commercials) before M2 (faculty) has happened. If faculty aren't onside, commercials will collapse in committee.

**Realistic elapsed time M1→signed pilot:**

- Private universities: 6–10 weeks.
- Autonomous DU/Jamia: 12–20 weeks.
- Central universities: don't plan for.

## 5. Leave-behind pack — what to have ready before your first meeting

You cannot go to a college pitch without these. If they don't exist yet, that is a **blocker for pitching**, not a nice-to-have.

### 5.1 Product one-pager (for the Dean to circulate)

Contents:

- One-line description
- What faculty do (three steps)
- What faculty own (their transcript, their assignments, discard rights)
- What the institution gets (lesson records for NAAC, absentee support, faculty admin reduction)
- What we do NOT do (grade students, replace teachers, sell data)
- Contact + institution namedrop (if truthful)

### 5.2 NAAC-alignment note (for the NAAC coordinator)

Cross-reference each GyanSaar output to specific NAAC criteria. Draft with someone who has been through a NAAC cycle. Placeholder mapping:

| GyanSaar output | NAAC criterion |
|---|---|
| Approved lesson records | 2.3 — Teaching-Learning Process |
| Assignment sets with instructor edits | 2.5 — Evaluation Process and Reforms |
| Transcript archive with search | 2.3.1 — Student-centric methods |
| Faculty consent records | 6.3 — Faculty Empowerment Strategies |

**Get this reviewed by a real NAAC coordinator before you circulate it.** A wrong criterion citation kills credibility instantly.

### 5.3 Data-protection brief (for IT / DPO / legal)

- Where audio is stored (Cloudflare R2, encrypted at rest, India region if we commit to this).
- Who can access what (RLS by institution — cite PRD §8.4a).
- What the client can do (approve, discard, export their own data).
- What we cannot do (read raw audio; only the automated system can — cite PRD §8.4a "asset/transcript return permission denied").
- DPDP-compliance posture — consent-first, instructor as data subject, no minor data captured intentionally.
- Data-retention policy (state it; if you don't have one yet, get it from Ankit before pitching).
- Sub-processors we use (Sarvam, OpenRouter, Supabase, Cloudflare) with links.

### 5.4 Pilot terms one-pager (for later meetings — M3 or M4)

Same shape as the coaching pilot terms, but adapted:

- **Scope:** one department, one semester, up to N faculty members, up to X hours of recording per faculty.
- **Cost:** free during pilot, subject to a written LOI.
- **Success criteria (jointly defined at pilot start):** X hours captured, Y approved lesson records, Z NAAC-usable artefacts, faculty NPS ≥ threshold.
- **Data ownership:** institution owns all transcripts and derived data.
- **Exit:** institution can walk away at pilot end, with a full export.
- **Continuation pricing:** placeholder — do not put a number here until the founder has decided.

## 6. Objection handling — the top twelve

### 6.1 "How is this different from surveillance? Our faculty won't accept it."

> "Surveillance is when the observer decides what happens with the recording. Here the instructor is the observer, the recorder, the approver, and the only party who can discard. If they discard, no metric measures that — the institution never sees they discarded. They own their own tape. May we come and hear the concerns of your faculty directly? That conversation is worth more to us than any assurance from me."

### 6.2 "How does this map to NAAC criteria?"

Hand them the note in §5.2. Do not answer from memory.

### 6.3 "We are due for NAAC re-accreditation in [X months]. Is this fast enough to help?"

> "That depends on when your submission window is. If you start a pilot within four weeks and run for a full semester, you will have both quantitative evidence (X approved lesson records across Y courses) and a qualitative process narrative for the NAAC submission. If your window is sooner than that, we should be honest — a shorter pilot won't produce enough evidence to move a criterion score."

### 6.4 "What integrations do you offer? We use Moodle / Google Classroom / MS Teams."

> "Direct integration — not yet. Every artefact we produce (transcript, assignment set, lesson record) is exportable in standard formats — plain text, PDF, CSV — that faculty can attach to any LMS activity in one step. Deep integration with Moodle is on our roadmap once we have three institutional customers using Moodle asking for it. If [College] wants to be one of those three, that becomes a real conversation."

### 6.5 "Who owns the transcripts and the recordings? The instructor, the college, or you?"

> "The instructor owns their transcripts and recordings for their teaching. The institution has a licence to those transcripts as internal documentation, matching how classroom teaching is treated in your existing service agreements with faculty. We — GyanSaar — hold nothing beyond what is required to run the service, and never re-use content for anything else. Written in our terms; happy to walk your legal counsel through it."

### 6.6 "Is your product empanelled on GEM? On any state education panel?"

> "Not yet — we are a young company. If empanellment is required for [College] to procure us, we would like to know that early so we can begin the process, which typically takes [research required — check with Ankit]. Meanwhile, we can start with a no-cost pilot without procurement being on the critical path."

### 6.7 "What is your pricing?"

> "Pricing depends on faculty count and hours-per-semester. To give you a real number rather than a range, I'd like to understand your department size and typical class hours. Or — if a directional range is more useful now — we are targeting a per-course-per-semester cost that is a fraction of what one hour of a visiting faculty's compensation would be. We are optimising for institutional adoption, not per-seat revenue."

### 6.8 "What if you go out of business?"

> "Two protections. First, every institution's data is exportable at any time in standard formats — no proprietary lock-in. Second, we can commit contractually to placing our core software in escrow with a third party if that matters to your legal team. We are a young company; we should not pretend otherwise. What we can control is that you are never held hostage by us."

### 6.9 "Have you had a data-security audit? SOC 2?"

> "We do not yet hold SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certification — those are on our roadmap and are on the critical path before we serve larger enterprises. We can provide our current security posture documentation, and can commit to any specific control your DPO requires during the pilot. This is a fair question and one we take seriously, not defensively."

### 6.10 "Can we white-label this as [College] property?"

> "For pilots, no — we want your faculty responding to the product, not the paint. If [College] becomes an ongoing customer, custom branding is a conversation we're happy to have. It is not a technical blocker; it is a decision we take once."

### 6.11 "Where is the audio stored? Is it in India?"

> "Storage is Cloudflare R2. Region can be specified as India (Mumbai) if that is a compliance requirement for [College]. Our default deployment is India-region for exactly this reason."

### 6.12 "Are you using our recordings to train your models?"

> "No. Our AI vendors (Sarvam for transcription, OpenRouter for language models) contractually do not use our data for training. Our own systems do not train on client audio. This is written in our terms and reflects a principle, not a preference — the value your institution generates in classroom is your intellectual property, not our training data."

## 7. Follow-up email templates

### 7.1 After M1 (same day, within 6 hours)

Subject: **GyanSaar — recap and materials from today's discussion**

> Dear Professor [Name],
>
> Thank you for the time this afternoon.
>
> As promised, please find attached the three documents we discussed: a product summary, a NAAC-alignment note, and a data-protection brief. You are welcome to share these with colleagues at [College] you feel should see them.
>
> You mentioned that [X — quote them back to themselves]. That is exactly the kind of concern our design is intended to address, and I would value the chance to hear it in more detail from your faculty directly. Would a follow-up conversation in the next two weeks, with two or three faculty members you'd nominate, be feasible?
>
> With regards,
> Deepak

### 7.2 If they've gone silent (T+14 days after M1)

Subject: **Following up on GyanSaar — helpful to hear even a 'not now'**

> Dear Professor [Name],
>
> I am writing briefly to check whether the materials I left were useful, and whether the follow-up conversation with faculty is something [College] can consider now, or whether the timing is not right.
>
> Either answer is genuinely helpful to us in planning our term. Would a two-line reply be feasible?
>
> With regards,
> Deepak

**Do not send a third follow-up.** If two go unanswered, the answer is no for now. Return in six months.

## 8. Rehearsal checklist before your first college meeting

- [ ] Read this document out loud once.
- [ ] Read the coaching pitch playbook (`Pitch-CoachingCentres.md`) — many objections are shared.
- [ ] Draft §5.1, §5.2, §5.3 leave-behind papers. **Do not go to M1 without them.**
- [ ] Have the NAAC-alignment note reviewed by one person who has actually done a NAAC cycle. Ask your college contacts — someone in your network has done one.
- [ ] Rehearse the demo 20 times. Same rules as coaching — hotspot, charge, backup recording.
- [ ] Dress: formal — dark trousers, ironed shirt, closed shoes. No suit. No jeans.
- [ ] Bring printed leave-behind pack ×3 (Dean's copy, HOD's copy, spare).
- [ ] Bring a laptop for this segment — colleges expect it. Have the demo on a tablet or the phone, but the laptop is there for the leave-behind papers if requested electronically.
- [ ] Confirm meeting location 24 hrs before. Deans reschedule frequently; do not travel without confirming.

## 9. Timeline and success metrics for college segment (first 6 months)

| Milestone | Timeline | Success signal |
|---|---|---|
| First cold outreach | Week 1 | 20 emails sent, 3 replies |
| First M1 (discovery + demo) | Week 3 | 1 meeting completed, materials left behind |
| Three M1s | Week 6 | 3 champions engaged with materials |
| First M2 (faculty room) | Week 8 | Surveillance objection surfaced and addressed publicly |
| First M3 (IT / DPO) | Week 10 | Compliance sign-off in principle |
| First LOI signed | Week 12 | Pilot begins |
| First pilot midpoint | Week 20 | 40+ approved lesson records, faculty NPS collected |
| First pilot end + go/no-go | Week 24 | Renewal decision from institution |

If you're at Week 6 with zero M1s, the wedge isn't landing — come back to §0 and rediagnose. Do not push harder on the same message.

## 10. What to do after each college meeting

Within 4 hours, in your CRM/sheet:

- Who attended (all of them, roles).
- What criteria they cited (NAAC, NIRF, autonomy, procurement, etc.).
- Who among the room was the strongest supporter, who was the strongest skeptic.
- What internal process they described as next (staff council, purchase committee, faculty senate).
- What they asked for that you could not answer. **This is the most important field.** Get answers to these before the next meeting.

**After three M1s across three different colleges:** come back to this file and edit anything that didn't land or that produced a consistent objection you don't have a good answer for.
